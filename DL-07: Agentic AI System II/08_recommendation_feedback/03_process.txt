Module: Recommendation and Feedback
Process and techniques

The recommendation is the final product shown to the user. It must be clear, useful, and honest about uncertainty. The user should quickly understand whether to travel normally, change route, delay, or avoid travel.

Recommended response fields
- action_code, risk_level, and confidence
- short_summary and immediate_actions
- primary_route and alternatives with trade-offs
- emergency_instructions and official_contacts
- reasons and source citations
- observed_at, fetched_at, and expires_at
- limitations and degraded_services
- request_id and version metadata

Key techniques
- Use short action sentences and place urgent steps first.
- Use alert deduplication and cooldown to reduce notification fatigue.
- Never hide an alert when its severity becomes higher.
- Classify feedback as helpful, incorrect, stale, unsafe, route issue, or source issue.
- Sanitize, label, and review feedback before using it for model training.
- Measure safety metrics such as HIGH-risk recall, unsafe recommendation rate, and alert latency.
- Measure user-experience metrics separately.
- Use canary or A/B tests only for presentation changes, not for weaker safety thresholds.
- Apply data minimization, access control, retention, export, and deletion rules to location history.

Success means the advice is timely, supported by evidence, easy to act on, and connected to a controlled improvement process.
